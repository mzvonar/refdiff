**The reframe in one paragraph.** Mato asked whether handling divergence would dilute the
fine-detail polishing refdiff was built for. Measured: steps 1 and 2 are inert above the confidence
floor (`unverified` = 0 on all five pairs at confidence ≥ 0.5), but steps 3 and 4 as written are not

measurement. So the work splits into a model-driven **`reconcile`** (read both, fix the structure)
and today's loop as **`polish`**, and what the matcher owed `reconcile` was honesty, not cleverness.

### 2. ~~Plan step 4 — say which PHASE a pair is in~~ **DONE 2026-09-15**

**52 of 52 pairs carry a phase — 28 `polish` / 24 `reconcile` — and no finding changed anywhere**
(7201 findings across the three corpora, `+0 / −0` on every pair). By corpus: refdiff 8/1,
uctoinak2 6/23, uctoinak2-storybook **14/0** — all 14 component pairs `polish`, which is the outcome
step 3 existed to protect. Full write-up in the plan's step-4 DONE block; the derivation lives in
`packages/core/src/structural/phase.ts`'s file comment. What a next session needs at hand:

- **The rule that shipped is neither of the plan's two candidates, and the reason is the thing to
  carry forward: BOTH of them read the wrong confidence.** `pairPhase` reads
  **`max(confidenceX, confidenceY)`**, not the joint `alignment.confidence`, plus match rate ≥ 0.70.
  The joint score counts an anchor only when BOTH axes land it, so it collapses to 0.00 on a surface
  that corresponds along one axis — `tx-picker-owner-mobile` has 17 anchors, **14 agreeing on X, 1
  on Y, 0 on both**, at match rate 0.91. Both candidates would have sent the corpus's
  best-corresponding component pairs off to "reconcile the structure".
- **The plan's hypothesis was half right.** The fitter is NOT failing spuriously: that pair's Y
  relation is genuinely non-affine (pairwise slopes −0.31 … 2.33; the best possible affine Y fit
  lands 8 of 17 anchors). What is wrong is reading the joint score for this question at all.
  `Alignment.confidenceX`'s own doc comment already said the collapse happens and that the joint
  score is right **for the pixel gate, because pixels need both axes**. A phase is not a pixel diff.
  *(Compounding it: that pair's Y fit also bailed at a median |residual| of 12.10 px against
  `AXIS_RESIDUAL_MAX` = 12 — a 0.8% miss that costs `confidenceY` 0.47 → 0.06 and still reports
  `basis: anchors`. A 0.00 can be a threshold cliff rather than a measurement.)*
- **`textShare` is reported and does NOT gate.** Weakest signal against actual mis-pairing (Spearman
  −0.44, vs −0.71 for match rate), and its 0.35 threshold separates nothing: median junk 0.310 just
  below it, 0.313 just above. The plan's rule B reaches the right verdict for the wrong reason.
- **The threshold's own caveat, so nobody re-derives it as news:** `MIN_AXIS_CONFIDENCE` currently
  excludes nothing `MIN_MATCH_RATE` did not — on this corpus `rate >= 0.70` alone gives the same
  28/24 partition. It is kept because the nearest pair sits EXACTLY on 0.50
  (`client-pending-accountant-mobile`) and because it is live below the rate floor (13 of 24 clear
  it). Until a pair fires it, **"no single number detects the phase" is a claim this corpus does not
  demonstrate.**
- **Reported, never enforced, and pinned as such.** `phase.test.ts` carries a source ratchet: the
  label may be mentioned only in the five reporting seams (`types.ts`, `cli.ts`,
  `package-for-model.ts`, `summary.ts`, `phase.ts`), with a guard case so it cannot pass vacuously.
  The target it guards is `structural/checks.ts`, which already takes `alignmentConfidence` — adding
  a phase argument there is a one-line change that reads like an improvement and would quietly stop
  emitting findings on divergent pairs.
- Two numbers in the plan were wrong and are corrected in place: the six disputed pairs include
  **three** Storybook pairs, not four; and "29 of 34 below confidence 0.5" re-derives to **32 of 52**.

### 3. Plan step 5 — refuse geometric pairs below the confidence floor ← DO NEXT

Old step 3's outcome at a fraction of its risk, and the plan's own stated fix direction as a
threshold rather than a rewrite. Below 0.5, form no geometric pairs; report missing/extra. **The
acceptance criterion is that no pair at confidence ≥ 0.5 changes by a single finding.**

which is currently phrased in terms of the joint score.

### PARKED (full reasoning in the plan)

- **Container-scoped matching with sequence alignment** — the original step 3, **not scheduled**.
  Largest change in the plan, re-decides 219 geometric matches on the known-good pairs, and removes
  the property the polish loop depends on: that a globally-unique text matches ANYWHERE. The part
  worth keeping is the container correspondence as a `reconcile` **output** (a structure map), built
  beside the matcher, never inside it — an output cannot misfire the way a confidence-gated code
  path can.
- **Lowe's ratio test** — the original step 4, **dropped as a gate**. Structurally hostile to any
  layout where position is the only discriminator and positions repeat uniformly: measured exposure
  `refdiff-library-groups-mobile`, 150 geometric matches at confidence 0.70, text share 0.13. (174
  of its 243 geometric findings are on `text`-role elements, so this is NOT the textless-box case it
  first looks like — a grid cell whose text differs from the comp's falls to geometry and its
  runner-up is the next row.) Redundant in `reconcile`, where the pair is already untrustworthy
  wholesale. **What survives:** `Finding.ambiguityMargin` as a reported diagnostic — compute it, put
  it on the finding, never refuse a pair on it.
- The counterpart-widening idea step 0 reverted may pay once the corpus grows. The bar to beat
  is recorded in the plan: −2 findings, +1 match, witness still alive — i.e. marginal.

  major / 63 minor), **34 flagged `unverified`**, pairings 49 text / 3 slot / 71 geometry / 102 on
  no pair.
- **Baseline before thresholds.** Steps 3–5 all make the matcher refuse more pairs, and refusal
  converts into missing + extra. Step 2's corpus table is what tells "more precise" from "failed
  differently".
- **The corpus, after step 3:** three corpora — `refdiff` (9), `uctoinak2` (31 routes),
  `uctoinak2-storybook` (14 components). **20 pairs at confidence ≥ 0.5, carrying 745 geometric
  matches of 1576.** The pre-step-3 figures (34 pairs, 5 over the floor, 219 geometric of 463) are
  superseded and two of them were measured against a stale annotator — see the step-3 block in the
  plan before quoting anything from the earlier baseline.
- **The matcher was the wrong instrument for divergence** — the reframe's core claim, and the one to
  re-read before any future "make matching cleverer" idea. refdiff exists because the model cannot
  see a 2 px offset or a ΔE 3 colour delta; in the divergent case the hard thing is NOT invisible to
  the model ("the comp draws a thread rail with relative dates, the impl renders absolute dates and
  a filter chip row" is something a model reading both sources gets right). Divergence needs
  structural JUDGEMENT, which no measurement supplies. What the matcher owed that case was honesty
  (steps 1–2), not cleverness. Hence `reconcile` (model-driven) / `polish` (today's loop). Those two
  were once called phase A and phase B; the letters are retired, because step 4's candidate RULES
  are lettered nowhere either — they are named for the confidence each reads.
- **A phase must never be implemented as a GATE on the existing pipeline.** A threshold switch
  misfires and the corpus already holds the pair that trips it: `today-owner-desktop` scores
  confidence **0.50** — over any sane floor — at match rate **0.31**, the worst in the corpus.
  Phase-A work belongs in outputs that cannot touch leaf matching.
- **No single number detects the phase.** `alignment.confidence` says "can a transform be fitted";
  match rate (`matched / min(designLeaves, implLeaves)`) says "do the two sides contain the same
  things"; text share (`matchedVia.text / matched`) says "is correspondence PROVEN or assumed from
  position". Counter-examples: `messages-owner-desktop` 0.07 confidence at rate 0.68;
  `today-owner-desktop` 0.50 at 0.31; `refdiff-library-groups-mobile` rate 0.79 at share 0.13. The
  last two are computable only because step 2 added `ComparisonReport.matching`.
- **A ratio test is hostile to repeated-content layouts by construction** — where position is the
  only discriminator and positions repeat uniformly, the runner-up is the next row. NOT the
  textless-box case it first looks like: **174 of `refdiff-library-groups-mobile`'s 243 geometric
  findings are on `text`-role elements** (a grid cell whose text differs from the comp's falls out
  of text matching into geometry). Checked before parking step 4, and it corrected the reason.
- **A pair that fails to CAPTURE leaves no run dir, so it is invisible to `summary`.** The set
  summary renders a complete-looking table over a shrunken corpus, every row in it correct. That
  was 20 of the 54 declared pairs before step 3 and is **2** after it (the known `docs-owner-*`
  404s) — the other 18 were a stale annotator, a cold Vite, and one missing Storybook mock export,
  none of which announced itself. The baseline harness names them because nothing else can, and
  that naming is the only reason any of the three was found.
- **Instrument before change.** Step 1 came first because a raw finding count cannot evaluate a
  matcher change — the veto widening produced −10/+8 and told us almost nothing.
- **The gate FLAGS, it does not suppress.** A suppressed finding cannot be told from "no difference
  here" — the same bug with its sign flipped. The pixel channel had already settled this next door:
  below its floor it does not go quiet, it emits one finding SAYING it skipped
  (`lowConfidenceFinding` in `pixel/checks.ts`). Measured cost of the alternative on the witness:
  67 of the 225 findings are value findings, so suppressing on confidence alone deletes all 67, and
  suppressing only what the text exemption leaves still deletes 34 a reader could never know had
  been raised.
- **An AGGREGATE score answers the question it was BUILT for, not the one you are asking.**
  `alignment.confidence` is agreement on both axes at once, which is right for the pixel gate and
  wrong for the phase — 17 anchors, 14 on X, 1 on Y, 0 on both, and the score reads 0.00 about a
  pair that matches 91% of its leaves. The warning was already in `Alignment.confidenceX`'s own doc
  comment; nobody had asked whether the new consumer shared the pixel channel's requirement. Read
  what a score aggregates before reusing it. Also: `fitAxis` bails to the identity above a 12 px
  median residual and says nothing — that pair's is **12.10**, and `basis` still reads `anchors`.
- **Alignment confidence is evidence about a PAIRING only where the pairing came from geometry.**
  A `via: "text"` pair carries the same string on both sides, so it is evidence about itself and
  the transform played no part in forming it — exempt at any confidence. This is the one place
  step 1 deliberately sharpened the plan's wording ("gate on alignment confidence"). On the witness
  the 67 value findings split **33 text-proven / 1 slot / 33 geometric**, so the exemption is the
  difference between flagging half the set and all of it.
- **`position` and `spacing` are NOT gated.** They carry `via`/γ so a reader can weigh them, but a
  position finding IS the transform's own claim and states its evidence in its own message ("offset
  by (-70.7, 0.5)px" is visibly not drift); a colour delta offers no such tell.
- **`identityKey` must keep ignoring the provenance fields.** It is built from type + role +
  expected/actual + text, deliberately without geometry, so a human triage decision survives a
  recapture. Adding `via`/`gamma`/`unverified` to it would re-key every finding and orphan every
  decision.
- **`messages-accountant-desktop` captures a thread deep link** because comp frame 1c opens thread
  t2 (`messages.dc.html` state `sel: { od: 0, ad: 1 }`); a bare route opens t1 and the whole
  conversation reports twice over as missing + extra.
- Repo design principles that bind this work are in `CLAUDE.md`: the model is never the comparator,
  pure stages with effects only in adapters, one bad pair must never kill a run, and **suppression
  is visible or it does not happen** — which is the house rule the flag-don't-suppress decision
  above is an instance of.

## Env gotchas

- **This box cannot run two dev servers and a capture browser.** 7 GB, of which a warm Next dev
  server holds ~3.4 GB after compiling the uctoinak2 routes. Starting Storybook beside it (to pick
  up the 14 component pairs) OOM-killed the app server mid-corpus — `dmesg -T | grep -i oom` named
  it outright, after a detour through "did the harness break?". A later full run was killed by the
  task supervisor for the same reason while it was ALSO running `pnpm -r build`. Practical rules:
  `free -m` and `dmesg -T | grep -i oom` before theorising; `--no-build` when dist is known current;
  and measure the corpora in separate passes (`--only <corpus>`) — the harness keeps each corpus's
  last numbers in the document, dated, so a split run still produces a whole baseline.
- **`svc down <name>` from a worktree that does not declare that unit can stop a DIFFERENT one.**
  `svc down annotator-tailnet` run from the uctoinak2 worktree stopped that worktree's ad-hoc
  `design-live` instead — the unit I needed. Run `svc down` from the checkout that owns the unit,
  and check `svc status` after, not before only.
- **A child process's errors are on STDERR, and a stdout-only capture reports a clean run.** The
  first version of the baseline harness piped only stdout, so `compare`'s `console.error` typed
  capture failures never reached it and the document said "nothing missing" about a run with four
  dead pairs. Exactly the failure the whole workstream is about — a report that looks complete —
  reproduced in the tool built to detect it.
- **refdiff has NO formatter of its own, but the editing hook in a uctoinak2-rooted session does.**
  Every Write/Edit fires uctoinak2's `PostToolUse` hook — `oxlint --fix` then `oxfmt --write` — on
  the file just written, regardless of which repo it belongs to. refdiff is mid-migration on
  semicolons (36 of 90 source files still carry them, 54 do not), so the output is where the repo
  is going, but a three-line change to one of the 36 arrives as a few hundred lines of punctuation.
  **Commit the reformat separately FIRST** (see `73cc4a1`): back the files up, `git checkout --`
  them, run the two commands by hand from `/root/uctoinak2`, verify formatted-HEAD is
  behaviour-identical (typecheck + the parent's exact test counts), commit as `style:`, then
  restore and commit the real change. Phase 1's diff went from 1163+/594− to 480+/13− doing this.
- **A number written in a handoff is not a measurement.** Phase 1 started from "expect 448 core"
  and "the count must equal 223" — both wrong (445 and 225), because the previous handoff recorded
  run 13's figures, and run 13 still HAD the widening step 0 removes. The plan's own "225 → 223"
  line said so one paragraph away. **Re-measure the baseline, and when recording one, name the
  run it came from.** Both lessons are in `docs/lessons-inbox.md` (2026-09-15).
- **The CLIs run from `dist`.** A source edit is invisible until `pnpm -r build` (or `pnpm dev`'s
  watch). A compare run against a stale dist reports +0/−0 and is indistinguishable from a change
  that did nothing.
- **And `pnpm -r build` is NOT enough: every SERVER on both sides must be newer than the `dist` it
  runs.** The annotator `svc` unit had been up since before a build; `cli.ts` does
  `import { renderAppShell } from "./app-shell.js"`, so the process holds that module from the
  moment it started and a rebuilt dist never reaches it. It does not fail loudly — it serves a
  shell the source no longer describes, four pairs die `selector-not-found`/`step-failed`, and the
  pairs that DO capture report confident findings about a build nobody has. That state stood for a
  day and was written into the plan twice as "annotator-surface drift". `svc restart annotator`
  before a measurement; `skills/refdiff/preflight.sh` already checks for it.
- **A capture failure is evidence about the ENVIRONMENT, not a diagnosis.** All four of the above
  had a plausible per-pair story attached to them in the plan. The test that killed all four stories
  at once was one browser visit to the failing route.
- **A dev server that was OOM-KILLED leaves a poisoned Turbopack dist dir.** Afterwards every page
  and API route answers 500 while middleware-level redirects still answer 307, and the log shows
  only an `Edge Instrumentation` warning that was ALSO present during the last run that worked — so
  it reads like a cause and is not. `rm -rf .next-design` and restart. Do log-archaeology second.
  **And the readiness probe this handoff used to prescribe — `curl` a page route, expect 307 — is
  the one check the poisoned state still PASSES**, because 307 is the middleware redirect. It
  reported READY on a server that then failed all 31 pairs with `auth-failed: POST
  /api/test/session → HTTP 500`. Probe the route the capture actually needs:
  `curl -X POST -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"email":"nobody@example.com"}'
  http://127.0.0.1:3210/api/test/session` — a **404** (no such user) proves it compiled and ran; a
  500 proves nothing did. Warming is slow after an `rm -rf`, so poll it rather than sleeping once.
- **`pnpm storybook` hardcodes `-p 6006`, so `svc`'s `$PORT` is ignored** and `svc status` reports a
  port (6007) where nothing is listening. Probe 6006 regardless of what `svc status` says.
- **`svc` needs `XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`** in non-interactive shells. Fixed in `/root/svc` `73e5d32`; on a
  box without that commit, `export XDG_RUNTIME_DIR=/run/user/$(id -u)` before any `svc` call, or it
  dies with "Failed to connect to bus: No medium found" and looks like systemd is broken.
- **The devbox shells get a snapshot env**, so `~/.bashrc` edits do not reach the current session.
- **`pnpm x` becomes `node x`** — a killed wrapper leaves the real worker alive. Verify with
  `ps -eo pid,etimes,args` before starting anything on the same port.
- **Never `git checkout --` a file carrying uncommitted work.** Done once on `uctoinak2`'s
  accountant Messages page; restored from a full read earlier in the same context, which is luck,
  not a method. (This was written as "someone ELSE's uncommitted work" — wrong on the ownership,
  right on the rule. The `messages-redesign` worktree is ours; the WIP in it is this workstream's
  own from earlier sessions, so we may edit and commit there. Destroying uncommitted work is no
  more acceptable for being our own.)
- `/root/refdiff` and `/root/svc` are clean git repos. `CLAUDE.md`: **do not commit unless asked**;
  nothing is pushed. (Phase 1's commits were asked for explicitly.)
- **Three diagnoses were wrong before the real one** (fixture data; a `cacheComponents`/Suspense
  theory; "same text ⇒ trustworthy pair"). Each survived a plausible argument and died on a
  measurement. `docs/method.md` is the standing rule: every claim of progress is a number from
  `findings.json` or the corpus table, never an eyeball.
