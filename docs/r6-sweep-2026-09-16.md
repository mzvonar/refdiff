# STEP 6 — per-container confidence is REFUTED, and the defect it was aiming at is elsewhere

2026-09-16, with Mato. The measurement phase AND the verdict of **step 6**, the last step of
[`plan-divergent-matching.md`](plan-divergent-matching.md). Read-only over all 52 recorded pairs —
no capture, no server, no library change.

**Headline.** Per-container confidence does not survive its own corpus. It fails on
AVAILABILITY (460 of 1017 gated-eligible findings sit in no usable container), it fails its own
DONE criterion (at its best configuration three `polish` pairs get *worse*, one of them by 17 of
its 22 value findings), and where the corpus carries labels the local score is ANTI-predictive —
it catches 5 of 6 known-wrong pairings by breaking 43 of 79 known-correct ones. A
container-free version of the same idea is worse still, which rules out "containers are
unavailable" as the explanation and leaves "locality does not discriminate".

**What the measurement DID find is a different, unrefuted defect, in the opposite direction:**
`isUnverified` reads the JOINT alignment confidence — the reading `pairPhase` was deliberately
moved off — and **21 of 52 pairs sit below the floor on the joint score while at or above it on
their better axis, carrying 470 flagged findings between them.** Nine are `polish` pairs at match
rate ≥ 0.71 carrying 212 flags. The obvious fix (read `max(confidenceX, confidenceY)`) is
refuted here too, by the plan's own canonical witness. It is filed, not built.

---

## Method, and what makes it admissible

Step 6 changes `isUnverified` and nothing else, so **no pairing moves and `matched` cannot move**:
the whole effect is a set of `unverified` FLAGS, and that set is computable from each run's
recorded `elements.json` + `alignment` + `findings`. Two scripts, both read-only with respect to
every run dir, neither needing a server:

```bash
node scripts/r6-containers.ts [--pair <name>] [--out <file>.md]   # the corpus-wide cost
node scripts/r6-score.ts [--out <file>.md]                        # the falsification, against labels
```

**Three** admissibility gates, all met — the third because every number below is a DELTA against
today's gate, so the model of today's gate has to reproduce it:

- The anchor reconstruction re-implements `uniqueTextIndex` + `scoreAgreement` against the ALIGNED
  trees, so it must reproduce each run's recorded `alignment.confidence` / `confidenceX` /
  `confidenceY` to 4 dp. **52 of 52 reproduce, 0 dropped.**
- The text-pair evidence set comes from re-running the shipped `matchElements`, so it must
  reproduce that pair's recorded `matching` block. **52 of 52 reproduce, 0 dropped.**
- Re-deriving today's flag from `alignment.confidence < 0.5` must reproduce the recorded
  `unverified` on every finding. **It disagrees on 0 of 1017.** Without this the "newly flagged" and
  "newly cleared" columns would be deltas against a reconstruction rather than against the run.

### What a local confidence IS here, and the four choices it forces

`estimateTransform` scores the global fit as the fraction of unique-text anchors it explains within
`AGREE_PX` (10), damped by `min(1, anchors/8)/anchors`. A local score asks the same question of a
subset. Four axes were swept rather than picked, because each is a decision the plan's Step 6
block leaves open:

| axis | options | why it is not obvious |
| --- | --- | --- |
| evidence set | `anchors` (888 in the corpus) · `textpairs` (1424) | the anchor set is what the global score fits and it is SPARSE; text-proven pairings are denser and are what `isUnverified` already trusts |
| reading | `global` (residual under the fitted transform) · `refit` (residual after subtracting the container's own median offset) | `global` judges a pairing by the transform that actually formed it; `refit` asks whether the container is INTERNALLY coherent — a card that moved as a block whose contents still correspond |
| damping | shipped `min(1, n/8)/n` · undamped | the shipped damping is a penalty for a container being SMALL, not for the fit being bad: it caps a 3-evidence container at 0.375, below the 0.5 floor however perfectly the transform explains it |
| no-container fallback | `global` · `trust` · `distrust` | **the step's own first question**, and the only principled answer is `global` — the other two decide 45% of the population by fiat |

`refit` at min evidence 1 is degenerate by construction (one residual minus its own median is zero,
so the container always scores 1.00) and is reported only to show that it is.

## 1. Availability — the same limit that killed containment, one level down

**1017 gated-eligible findings** (a value type on a `via` ≠ text pairing); today's global gate
flags **644** of them.

| evidence set | in the corpus | findings whose container holds ≥1 | ≥3 | ≥8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `anchors` | 888 | 470 | 233 | 74 |
| `textpairs` | 1424 | 557 | 388 | 163 |

**460 of 1017 have no usable container under either evidence set.** Under the shipped damping,
**396 of the 470** anchor-backed ones sit in a container with fewer than 8 anchors, so their local
confidence is capped at `n/8` — below the floor for every n < 4 no matter how well the transform
explains them.

This is `docs/r3-sweep-2026-09-16.md`'s containment refutation reappearing as a different feature:
there the container column was absent on 8 of the 12 wrong pairs; here it is absent on 45% of the
population the gate acts on. **`reconcile.md` §R2 already states the rule — a placement report
states its own miss rate — and the miss rate is the whole answer both times.**

**The container that gets distrusted is the one holding 20 text-proven pairings** — the container
with the most PROOF of correspondence in the pair. Positional agreement inside a reflowing list is
low precisely because the list reflowed, and reflow is what a comp-vs-implementation comparison is
made of.

That single pair suggested a clean mechanism — *a local agreement score reads reflow, not
correspondence* — and the corpus does NOT confirm it as a monotone pattern, which is recorded here
rather than quietly dropped:

What the table does establish is worse for the proposal than the mechanism would have been: **the

four buckets.** A quantity that neither converges with sample size nor separates its own
population is not a measurement of anything.

## 4. The falsification, against labels the hypothesis did not come from

Scored the way step 5's containment hypothesis was: against the 107 labels of
`docs/r3-sweep-2026-09-16.md`, not against the pairs that suggested the rule. **85 of the 92 γ
candidates still exist** (step 5's slot ceiling refused 7); of the 12 labelled `wrong`, **6
survive**.

| population | n | container with ≥1 text pair | ≥3 | median local (global) | median local (refit) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| wrong | 6 | 5 | 1 | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| correct | 79 | 57 | 46 | 0.22 | 0.33 |

| reading | min ev | floor | wrong caught | correct broken | pixels-only caught / broken |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| global | 1 | 0.5 | 5 of 6 | **43 of 79** | 3 of 4 / 1 of 9 |
| global | 3 | 0.5 | 1 of 6 | 33 of 79 | 1 of 4 / 1 of 9 |

Every row costs more than it buys, and the `global` reading catches its 5 for a reason that is not
a discriminator at all — **five of the six wrong pairings sit in a container holding exactly ONE
text pair**, so the verdict is a single coin toss, and the same coin breaks 43 correct pairings.
The `refit` reading turns that same 1-evidence container into a score of **1.00** and therefore
**CLEARS the plan's canonical second witness** (`Vybavené` ↔ `Vybavené`, γ 1062.6) by construction.

### The honest limit of this half, stated rather than hidden

The labelled set is the LONG-γ band, which is by construction where genuine relocations live, and
**5 of the 6 surviving wrong pairings are `via: "text"` — exempt from this gate entirely.** The
labelled population the gate could actually act on is n = 1. So section 4 corroborates; it does
not carry the refutation. **Sections 1–3 do, and they are unbiased: they measure all 1017
gated-eligible findings.**

### Is it containers, or is it locality? — the complement of this section's own rule

Containers could be the whole problem: `containersOf`'s 64 px floor and 0.7 frame-share ceiling
place nothing on a flat page of same-size cards. So the same score was computed **without
containers** — the 8 text-proven pairings nearest the candidate, defined on 83 of 85 scorable
candidates against 62 for the container reading:

| reading | floor | wrong caught | correct broken | pixels-only caught / broken |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| global | 0.5 | 5 of 6 | **69 of 79** | 3 of 4 / **9 of 9** |
| refit | 0.5 | 3 of 6 | 58 of 79 | 3 of 4 / 8 of 9 |

**Strictly worse**, and on the independent pixels-only subset it breaks **every** correct pairing
it is defined on. Availability was not the explanation. Locality does not discriminate.

## 5. Step 2's parked question, re-asked — and answered with a mechanism this time

> *Should an `unverified` finding count toward the verdict?*

Answered "yes, change nothing" at step 2 from the corpus: excluding the flagged findings flipped
ZERO of 52 verdicts. Step 6 was named as the first configuration where the two answers become
distinguishable, because a pair could have its only gating findings inside one bad container.

**It does not, in any of the 24 configurations swept — still 0 of 52.** And the count is no longer
the answer; the reason is:

> **51 of 52 pairs fail.** Across them **4782 findings decide the verdict**, and the flag can reach
> **616 of them — 12.9%**. On **0** pairs does it reach all of them.

A verdict is decided by `missing-element` / `extra-element` presence findings and by text-proven
pairings, and **no version of this flag touches either**. The question is not close on this corpus;
it is unanswerable by construction, and it stops being a parked question.

Step 2's closest pair, `settings-accountant-desktop`, reproduces to the number — 41 gating, 7
reachable, 7 flagged, 34 standing — and is not close either, because **every one of the 34 is out
of the flag's reach by TYPE**: 18 `missing-element`, 3 `extra-element`, 8 `position`, 1 `spacing`,
and 4 value findings on text-proven pairings. Nothing a confidence gate can say about a pairing
touches any of them.

## 6. What the measurement found instead — filed, not built

`isUnverified` reads `alignment.confidence`, the JOINT score, which counts an anchor only when it
agrees on BOTH axes. `Alignment.confidenceX`'s own doc comment says the joint score is right FOR
THE PIXEL GATE because diffing pixels needs both axes — and `pairPhase` reads
`max(confidenceX, confidenceY)` for exactly that reason. **The gate still reads the joint one, and
step 4's DONE block already said a threshold should not.**

**21 of 52 pairs are below the floor on the joint score and at or above it on their better axis,
carrying 470 of the corpus's 644 flags.** The nine `polish` pairs among them:

| pair | match rate | joint | max(x, y) | gated | flagged today |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| refdiff-compare-mobile-toolbar-ghost | 0.92 | 0.46 | 0.99 | 5 | 5 |
| settings-owner-mobile | 0.75 | 0.25 | 0.88 | 5 | 5 |
| tx-picker-owner-mobile | 0.91 | 0.00 | 0.82 | 29 | 29 |
| tx-picker-accountant-mobile | 0.86 | 0.00 | 0.80 | 36 | 36 |
| settings-accountant-mobile | 0.75 | 0.38 | 0.75 | 9 | 9 |
| tx-picker-accountant-desktop | 0.86 | 0.42 | 0.74 | 24 | 24 |
| refdiff-gallery-desktop | 0.89 | 0.40 | 0.64 | 75 | 75 |
| client-detail-chrome-accountant-mobile | 0.71 | 0.38 | 0.63 | 12 | 12 |
| settings-accountant-desktop | 0.78 | 0.38 | 0.63 | 17 | 17 |

212 findings on nine pairs where 71–92% of leaves matched, every one flagged *this pair is not
worth believing*. `tx-picker-owner-mobile` is the documented case: joint **0.00** while
`confidenceX` fits at 0.82, and the handoff already records that its 0.00 is a threshold cliff (the
Y fit bailed at a median |residual| of 12.10 against `AXIS_RESIDUAL_MAX` 12), not a measurement.

**And the obvious fix is refuted by the plan's own canonical witness.** `messages-accountant-desktop`
reads joint 0.07 / max **0.50** — exactly the floor — so reading `max(confidenceX, confidenceY)`
would UNFLAG the pair step 1 was built for, including the four flagged findings of the
`Včera` ↔ `Otázky · 1` mis-pairing. A blanket switch trades 470 false flags for the witness.

So this is a located defect with a refuted first fix, which is a question and not a change. It
needs its own measurement phase, and the shape of it is already visible: the nine pairs above are
separated from the witness by MATCH RATE (0.71–0.92 against 0.51), which is the strongest signal in
the plan's own table — and which is also the signal a gate must never read, by the ratchet in
`phase.test.ts`. That tension is the next question, not this one's answer.

## What this leaves standing

- **Per-container confidence is closed.** Not "unpromising" — measured, on the corpus, against the
  criterion it set itself, in 24 configurations. Do not rebuild it; the container map's miss rate
  is the reason and it is a property of `containersOf`, which cannot conjure containers a flat
  layout does not have.
- **Locality is closed too**, by the container-free reading, which is the stronger statement and
  the reason the complement was audited at all.
- **The `via: "text"` mis-pairing family is still open** — 5 wrong of 79 long-γ text pairs, with
  no feature separating them (distance, axis, containment and now locality all refuted).
- **The slot family's recall is still unmeasured** — 22 of the 43 slot pairs step 5's ceiling keeps
  carry token-disjoint texts.
- **NEW: the gate reads the wrong confidence, and the right one is not `max`.** Section 6.
- `scripts/r6-containers.ts` and `scripts/r6-score.ts` have no tests, like
  `scripts/baseline-matching.ts` before them — `scripts/` is not a vitest project. Named rather
  than passed over; what stands in for a test is the two reproduction gates, which is why they
  report 52/52 and 0 dropped in their own output.
